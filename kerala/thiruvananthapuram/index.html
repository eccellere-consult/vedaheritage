Kerala · South Kerala

Thiruvananthapuram

Kerala's capital, a royal city between hills and sea

Thiruvananthapuram: Travancore's royal capital with Padmanabhaswamy Temple, Kovalam and Varkala beaches, museums and Kerala's government and IT hub.

Headquarters: Thiruvananthapuram

A brief history

The district grew around the Travancore kingdom, whose rulers dedicated the realm to Sree Padmanabha. Under Marthanda Varma the capital was fixed here in the 18th century, and it became the capital of the Indian state in 1956.

  1. Ancient

    Part of the Ay and later Venad kingdoms in the far south.

  2. 18th century

    Marthanda Varma consolidates Travancore and rebuilds Padmanabhaswamy Temple.

  3. 1956

    Becomes the capital of the newly formed Kerala state.

What makes Thiruvananthapuram unique

  • Royal Travancore architecture and museums
  • Two of Kerala's best-known beaches, Kovalam and Varkala
  • Western Ghats foothills with Ponmudi and Neyyar
  • A stronghold of classical arts and learning

Best time to visit

October to March

Cool, dry weather suits beaches and city sightseeing; February–March brings the Attukal Pongala.

Avoid: Heavy monsoon (June–August) for beaches

What to look for

  • Padmanabhaswamy Temple and the fort area
  • Napier Museum and Kuthiramalika
  • Balaramapuram handloom
  • Varkala cliffs and Kovalam sunrise

Highlights

  • Padmanabhaswamy Temple
  • Kovalam and Varkala beaches
  • Napier Museum
  • Ponmudi hills

Festivals & events

  • Attukal Pongala (Feb–Mar)
  • Onam week (Aug–Sep)
  • Navaratri (Sep–Oct)

Dates change yearly; confirm before travelling.

Crafts & local products: Balaramapuram handloom, Coir and palmyra craft.
