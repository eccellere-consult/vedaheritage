Thirunelli Mahavishnu Temple

Ancient Vishnu kshetram at the feet of Brahmagiri

Thirunelli is an ancient Maha Vishnu temple tucked in a valley of the Brahmagiri hills in north Wayanad; tradition links the site to Brahma and the Purana accounts, and the complex sits beside the sacred mountain spring Papanasini. ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirunelli_Temple))

Pilgrims come to perform bali tharpanam (vavu bali) and other ancestral rites at Papanasini; Kerala’s ritual calendar and local authorities routinely manage large pilgrim gatherings for those rites. ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/thalassery/pazhassi/tirunelli-temple))

The temple is also a natural base for treks into the Brahmagiri range (Pakshipathalam and Brahmagiri peaks). Treks start close to Thirunelli but need forest‑department permission and are seasonally restricted for safety and fire risk. ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/wayanad/pakshipathalam-brahmagiri-hills.php))

Visitors get a mix of ritual atmosphere, granite Kerala temple architecture (notable vilakku maadam and stone work) and immediate access to forest trails, birding and panoramic hill views after the monsoon. ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/thalassery/pazhassi/tirunelli-temple))

Highlights

  • Papanasini stream — site for bali tharpanam and ritual dips. ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/thalassery/pazhassi/tirunelli-temple))
  • Ancient Vilakku Maadam and traditional granite work in the temple complex. ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/thalassery/pazhassi/tirunelli-temple))
  • Gateway for Brahmagiri / Pakshipathalam treks (forest permits required). ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/wayanad/pakshipathalam-brahmagiri-hills.php))
  • Called 'Kashi of the South' in local tradition for its role in pitru karmas. ([dtpcwayanad.com](https://www.dtpcwayanad.com/destination/thirunelli-temple-wayanad?utm_source=openai))
  • High elevation (≈890 m) gives post‑monsoon views and comfortable hill weather. ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirunelli_Temple))

Background

Thirunelli sits in a forested valley on the Kerala side of the Brahmagiri hills and is dedicated to Lord Maha Vishnu. The temples and surrounding theerthams (holy waters) feature in several Puranic references and local tradition that describe Brahma discovering and consecrating the idol. ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirunelli_Temple))

What to see and do

Walk down to the Papanasini spring to observe or (if participating) perform bali tharpanam under priest guidance; the stream and the steps to it are the focal point for ancestral rites at Thirunelli. Large numbers arrive on Karkidaka Vavu (the new‑moon day in Karkidakam) and other ritual dates. Confirm arrangements with the temple or local authorities if you plan to perform rites. ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/thalassery/pazhassi/tirunelli-temple))

Combine the temple visit with a guided trek into the Brahmagiri hills — popular options include the Pakshipathalam caves and Brahmagiri peak. Treks start from near the temple or nearby forest camps; you must get permission from the Wayanad forest authorities and use an authorised guide. Trails can be slippery in the monsoon and some routes are seasonally closed. ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/wayanad/pakshipathalam-brahmagiri-hills.php))

Birdwatching, simple village walks and photography: post‑monsoon the valley is green, visibility improves and hill vistas open up — ideal for nature and culture photography.

Plan your visit

Temple timings listed by Kerala Tourism are approximately 5:30 AM–12:30 PM and 5:30 PM–8:30 PM; darshan is traditionally free but check local notices for special festivals and crowd arrangements. Arrive early on ritual days (Karkidaka Vavu and other Amavasya occasions) when pilgrim numbers surge. ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/thalassery/pazhassi/tirunelli-temple))

If you want to trek to Pakshipathalam or Brahmagiri, contact the Wayanad forest division for permits and an authorised guide; district tourism bodies sometimes arrange transport and guides on request. Note the forest department may close or restrict treks seasonally (for safety and fire‑risk management). ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/wayanad/pakshipathalam-brahmagiri-hills.php))

Roads are winding ghat routes; allow extra travel time from Mananthavady or Kalpetta and expect limited public transport — private car or hired taxi is the most flexible option. Confirm local accommodation and transport in advance, especially during festival periods.

Plan your visit

Best time
Post‑monsoon to late winter (October–February) — clearer trails, full but safer waterways and good weather for treks. ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/wayanad/pakshipathalam-brahmagiri-hills.php))
How to reach
Drive from Mananthavady (≈32 km) or Kattikkulam; roads are ghat and slow — allow extra time. Limited local buses serve Thirunelli; taxis or self‑drive give the best flexibility. ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirunelli_Temple))

Etiquette & good to know

  • If you plan bali tharpanam, confirm priest availability and ritual timings with the temple before you travel (large crowds on Vavu days). ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/thalassery/pazhassi/tirunelli-temple))
  • Obtain forest‑department permission and a guide before attempting Pakshipathalam/Brahmagiri treks; wear good footwear and carry rain protection in shoulder seasons. ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/wayanad/pakshipathalam-brahmagiri-hills.php))
  • Respect temple dress codes and local customs; keep the Papanasini area clean and follow instructions during crowded rituals.
  • Avoid monsoon months for treks — trails are slippery and some routes are restricted; check current local notices before setting out. ([keralatourism.org](https://www.keralatourism.org/wayanad/pakshipathalam-brahmagiri-hills.php))
