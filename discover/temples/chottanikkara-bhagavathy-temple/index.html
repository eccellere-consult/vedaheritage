Chottanikkara Bhagavathy Temple

ചോറ്റാനിക്കര ഭഗവതി ക്ഷേത്രം

Major Devi shrine near Kochi, famed for its living ritual calendar and traditional healing (Keezhukkavu Guruthi).

Chottanikkara is a working Hindu temple complex in Ernakulam district, southeast of Kochi. The main sanctum (Melekkavu) houses the Goddess worshipped as Rajarajeswari; ritual life is active throughout the day and year, and the complex includes the lower Keezhukkavu (Bhadrakali) shrine, temple tank and pilgrim facilities.

Devotees visit for daily pujas, bhajans and the Keezhukkavu Guruthi (the ritual most closely associated with the temple’s healing traditions), and for seasonal festivals such as Makam Thozhal and Navaratri. The temple is administered by the Cochin Devaswom Board and operates official counters and an online booking portal for vazhipadu/poojas.

Highlights

  • Melekkavu (upper/main sanctum) — the Rajarajeswari sanctum where the deity is worshipped through three daily forms.
  • Keezhukkavu (lower Bhadrakali shrine) — focal point for the Guruthi ritual and the local healing tradition.
  • Makam Thozhal and the annual Ulsavam — major festival events (Kumbham; dates published annually by the temple).
  • Temple tank and the old pala tree — traditional features of the complex, linked in local accounts to pilgrims and healing lore.

History & significance

Why Chottanikkara Bhagavathy Temple matters

One of central Kerala’s best‑known Devi shrines; locally famed for rites believed to relieve severe mental distress and for a continuously active festival and puja calendar.

  1. By tradition (undated)

    Local legends credit early discovery and worship of the goddess to devotional figures such as Vilwamangalam Swamiyar and to a tribal herdsman story that yielded the sacred stones.

  2. 10th century CE (as reported)

    Kerala Tourism states the temple 'dates back to the 10th century CE' as a conventional historical estimate for its origins.

  3. 1950

    The Cochin Devaswom Board becomes the statutory body administering many Cochin‑area temples; Chottanikkara appears among temples under its management.

  4. Contemporary

    The temple maintains a public annual festival calendar (Makam Thozhal / Ulsavam, Navaratri etc.) and posts notices and pooja/booking information on its official website.

Good to know

  • Administration: Chottanikkara Temple is administered by the Cochin Devaswom Board.
  • Deity and daily forms: The Goddess is worshipped as Saraswati in the morning, Lakshmi at noon and Durga/Bhadrakali in the evening.
  • Principal image: The central idol is described in temple sources as a self‑manifested laterite stone (often referred to in accounts as a Rudrāksha Śilā).
  • Keezhukkavu (lower shrine) and the nightly Valiya Guruthi are central to the temple’s local healing/exorcism traditions.

Legend & folklore

The Kannappan story and the discovery of the stones

By local tradition, a herdsman (often named Kannappan in retellings) lived in the forest where the temple now stands. After incidents involving his cattle and family, two sacred stones are said to appear at the site.

A saint or seer recognises the stones as the Goddess and Narayana; later Vilwamangalam Swamiyar is traditionally linked with selecting and consecrating the shrine. A second idol and the Keezhukkavu shrine acquire prominence in local healing rites.

The Makam Thozhal tradition holds that on the Makam nakshatra day each year the goddess appears in full regalia before devotees; this annual appearance is celebrated as part of the temple’s ulsavam.

Versions of the origin tale vary in local retellings and printed accounts; these paragraphs present the commonly cited traditional narrative rather than verifiable historical fact. Sources include the temple’s own history notes and visitor guides.

Spiritual experience

The inner journey

Visitors come for darshan, bhajanam and the temple’s living ritual calendar; many devotees seek blessings and relief through rites at the Keezhukkavu (Guruthi). The temple’s practices are presented by local authorities and guides as devotional and traditional.

Attend the Keezhukkavu Guruthi (Valiya Guruthi)

Observe the evening Guruthi ritual at the lower Bhadrakali shrine from public areas. The rite is associated in local tradition with healing and the dispelling of evil influences; follow temple staff and priest instructions and do not photograph ritual participants without permission. (See sources on ritual practice and visitor guidance.)

Morning Nirmalya darshan and Mandapathil Paattu

Many devotees attend early morning prayers when the deity is worshipped as Saraswati; devotional singing (Mandapathil Paattu) and bhajanam are regular devotional offerings. Attend quietly and join devotional singing only where invited.

Festival darshan (Makam Thozhal / Ulsavam)

Witnessing festival processions, ezhunnallippu and the Makam Thozhal darshan offers insight into the temple’s ritual calendar; these events draw large crowds—plan for crowd management and confirm dates before travel.

Best for: Devotees of the Devi tradition, visitors interested in Kerala temple ritual life and festival culture, and those researching traditional healing/exorcism practices in a devotional setting.

  • Respect ritual privacy: do not photograph or intrude on people participating in healing rites or Guruthi; follow priests’ and staff directions. (sensitive)
  • Follow dress and entry rules for inner enclosures; ask at the counters if you are unsure. (sensitive)
  • Use the temple’s official vazhipadu counters or the online booking portal for paid offerings; confirm availability and fees before paying. (sensitive)
  • If accompanying someone seeking ritual healing, follow temple staff guidance and avoid physical interference or confrontational behaviour. (sensitive)

Plan your visit

Best time
October–March for cooler, drier weather and the main festival season; Makam Thozhal (in Kumbham, roughly Feb–Mar) and Navaratri are the busiest. Confirm festival dates with the temple before travelling.
How to reach
Chottanikkara is southeast of central Kochi and reachable by road from Ernakulam, Tripunithura and Kochi airport. Frequent local buses and taxis connect Tripunithura / Ernakulam to Chottanikkara; nearest rail halts and the airport require a short road transfer.
Suggested time
Plan 1–2 hours for a standard visit; allow more time during festivals or if you attend Guruthi or other special rites.

Etiquette & good to know

  • Dress according to Kerala temple custom: many inner sanctum rules ask men to wear mundu/dhoti (often without a shirt) and women to wear saree, set‑mundu or other modest traditional dress; confirm at the temple counter. (sensitive)
  • Photography is restricted inside the sanctum and during many rituals; always ask permission and follow signage and staff directions.
  • Do not interrupt or attempt to photograph participants during Guruthi or healing ceremonies; offer respectful distance.
  • Use official temple vazhipadu counters or the temple’s booking portal for paid offerings; beware of unlicensed intermediaries. (sensitive)

Getting there: train, bus & more

🚆 By train
Tripunithura railway station — station code TRTR; commonly used for longer‑distance visitors to Chottanikkara (Tripunithura is commonly cited as roughly 6–7 km from Chottanikkara in tourism materials). (Source: Tripunithura station page and Kerala Tourism festival PDF.)
Chottanikkara Road railway station — station code KFE; a nearer local halt served by selected passenger/local trains. Confirm current train services before travel. (Source: Chottanikkara Road station page.)
🚌 By bus (KSRTC)
KSRTC and private buses run from Ernakulam and Tripunithura to Chottanikkara; local bus stops serve the temple precinct. Confirm schedules with KSRTC or local counters. (Source: Kerala Tourism and local visitor guides.)
✈ By air
Cochin International Airport (COK, Nedumbassery) — the nearest airport; Kerala Tourism materials list an approximate road distance of about 36.6 km. Travel time varies with traffic; confirm route in advance.
