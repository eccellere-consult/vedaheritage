Ashtamudi Lake and Munroe Island

അഷ്ടമുടി കായൽ

Kerala's second-largest lake and the gateway to Kollam's village waterways

Ashtamudi Lake is a palm-fringed, multi-armed estuary at Kollam, recognised as a wetland of international importance under the Ramsar convention. Its name is commonly read as 'eight cones' or 'eight arms'.

Munroe Island (Munroe Thuruthu) is a cluster of small islands reached by canoe, known for coconut groves, coir and quiet village canals; it is a quieter alternative to the busier backwater circuits.

Highlights

  • Munroe Island canoe tours
  • Coir-making and fishing villages
  • Sunset cruise from Kollam
  • Close to Veda Heritage and Amritapuri

History & significance

Why Ashtamudi Lake and Munroe Island matters

Kollam (Quilon) was one of the great ancient ports of the Malabar coast, trading with the Middle East and China, and the starting point of the Kollavarsham, the Malayalam calendar.

  1. 825 CE

    The Kollavarsham, the Malayalam calendar era, begins, traditionally linked with Kollam.

  2. 13th–14th century

    Marco Polo and Ibn Battuta describe Kollam as a busy port visited by merchants from many lands.

  3. 1502

    The Portuguese establish a trading post at Kollam; the Dutch and British follow in later centuries.

  4. Early 19th century

    Colonel John Munro, British Resident of Travancore and Cochin, is associated with the island that now bears his name.

  5. 2002

    Ashtamudi Lake is designated a Ramsar wetland of international importance.

Good to know

  • The Ashtamudi short-neck clam fishery is among the first in India to receive MSC sustainability certification.
  • The President's Trophy Boat Race is held on Ashtamudi Lake.
  • Kollam has long been a centre of the cashew and coir industries.

Legend & folklore

The Land the Sea Gave Back

Kerala's founding legend says the sage Parashurama threw his axe from the shore at Gokarnam, and the sea receded as far as it flew, revealing the land that became Kerala. Lakes and backwaters such as Ashtamudi are remembered in local telling as the places where the sea has not quite let go.

Fishing families around the lake recount stories of seasonal tides, the 'chakara' (mudbank) years when fish gather, and of lake spirits to be respected before a boat sets out.

The lake-spirit stories are oral and need collection and attribution from local fishing communities.

Spiritual experience

The inner journey

Ashtamudi's villages are close to some of Kerala's most distinctive living spiritual centres, including Amritapuri and the Oachira Parabrahma Temple.

Oachira Parabrahma Temple

Oachira, near Veda Heritage, is known as a temple without a fixed idol, open to all, where worship centres on the formless Parabrahma. It is famed for the Oachirakali and the Panthrandu Vilakku festival.

Amritapuri Ashram

The ashram of Mata Amritanandamayi (Amma) lies a short distance from the retreat, with daily meditation and seva. Check visitor guidelines before arriving.

Best for: Spiritual seekers who want community-based practice alongside quiet lake scenery.

  • Contact the ashram in advance about visitor rules.
  • Dress modestly and observe silence zones.
  • Take time on the lake at dawn.

Plan your visit

Best time
October to March.
How to reach
Kollam Junction railway station and the KSRTC stand are close to the Ashtamudi boat jetty; Munroe Island is about 25 km from Kollam by road or boat.
Suggested time
Half to full day

Etiquette & good to know

  • Hire local boatmen and community guides where possible.
  • Do not feed wildlife or leave plastics on the water.
  • Respect nesting areas and fishing zones.

Getting there: train, bus & more

🚆 By train
Kollam Junction (QLN) is the main station, close to the Ashtamudi boat jetty.
🚌 By bus (KSRTC)
KSRTC Kollam bus station is in the city centre; buses run to Alappuzha, Thiruvananthapuram and beyond.
⛴ By water
The State Water Transport Department's Kollam–Alappuzha ferry crosses Ashtamudi Lake and the backwaters; check the schedule.
✈ By air
Thiruvananthapuram (TRV) airport is roughly 65–70 km away.
