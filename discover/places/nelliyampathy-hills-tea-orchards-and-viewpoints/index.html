Nelliyampathy Hills

Tea‑clad Western Ghats plateau with misty viewpoints and orchard walks

Nelliyampathy is a plateau in the Western Ghats (Palakkad district) covered in tea, coffee, cardamom and orange plantations and dotted with dramatic viewpoints. The area is a working landscape — estates, small villages and forested ridges form the scenery.

Visitors come for Seetharkundu’s cliff views over the Palakkad plains, windswept rocks at Kesavanpara, seasonal waterfalls after the monsoon and quiet orchard walks among orange and vegetable farms. Estate lanes and low‑traffic roads make it a relaxed hill escape.

The ghat climb through Pothundy (the common entrance) passes hairpin bends and dense forest before opening onto the plateau; accommodation is mainly homestays and estate stays rather than large hotels.

Because the hills sit on the monsoon‑fed Western Ghats, weather and access can change quickly — check local advisories, forest/road status and estate permission before you travel.

Highlights

  • Seetharkundu viewpoint — panoramic drop to the Palakkad plains
  • Tea, coffee, cardamom and orange estates with orchard walks
  • Kesavanpara and other rocky viewpoints for sunrise and cloud‑scapes
  • Seasonal waterfalls and streams strongest just after monsoon
  • Scenic ghat drive via Pothundi with many hairpin bends
  • Quiet homestays and small estate accommodations

Background

Nelliyampathy (sometimes spelled Nelliampathi) is a cluster of estate villages and viewpoints on a Western Ghats plateau in Palakkad district. The landscape is formed by commercial plantations (tea, coffee, spices and orange gardens) interspersed with forest pockets and exposed granite outcrops.

Pothundi Dam and its road act as the usual approach to the plateau; viewpoints such as Seetharkundu and Kesavanpara have long been the area’s defining attractions.

What to see and do

Seetharkundu (Seethargundu) — a rocky spur with wide views over the Palakkad gap and plains; best at dawn or late afternoon when mist and light make dramatic layers. Kesavanpara offers another classic rock viewpoint and photo opportunities.

Orchard and estate walks — after the monsoon (September–November) the estate roads, orange groves and spice gardens are lush and pleasant for slow walks; always ask estate owners or managers before entering private plantation sections or photographing workers.

Waterfalls and streams — short trails and seasonal cascades appear after heavy rain; approach cautiously and avoid slippery ledges. Use local guides for longer forest routes or wildlife spotting.

Plan your visit

Best time: post‑monsoon into winter (November–February) for clear views, cooler days and enjoyable orchard walks. Monsoon months make the hills dramatic but can cause road closures and landslips — confirm access before you go.

How to fit it in: Nelliyampathy is roughly 60 km from Palakkad town by road (Pothundi–Nemmara route). The ghat road climbs through many hairpin bends before the plateau; drive carefully and allow extra time for slow sections.

Accommodation is mostly small estate homestays, bungalows and limited resorts; book ahead in winter weekends. Confirm permissions for estate trails, follow local safety advice for cliff viewpoints and avoid visiting waterfalls alone after heavy rain.

How to reach
About 60 km from Palakkad (drive via Nemmara/Pothundi ghat road). The common approach is through Pothundi Dam; ghat climb includes many hairpin bends and forested stretches — allow extra time and drive cautiously.

Etiquette & good to know

  • Check local road, forest and district advisories on the day you travel — access has been restricted after heavy rain in 2026.
  • Ask permission before entering private estate lanes or photographing estate workers.
  • Carry warm layers and a light rain jacket in winter; trails can be misty and damp in mornings.
  • Keep to marked viewpoints and avoid slippery rock edges after rain.
