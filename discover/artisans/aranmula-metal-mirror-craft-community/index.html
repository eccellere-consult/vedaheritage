Aranmula Kannadi (Aranmula metal mirror) craft community

The secret metal mirror craft of Aranmula — living heritage and temple tradition

Aranmula Kannadi is a rare front‑surface metal mirror made by a handful of traditional artisan (Vishwakarma/Viswabrahmana) families in Aranmula village, Pathanamthitta. The mirrors are polished on the front so they reflect without glass or silvering.

The craft links closely to the Aranmula Parthasarathy Temple and local river festivals; the alloy recipe and polishing techniques remain community knowledge passed down within families. The product has a Geographical Indication (GI) registration (2004–05).

Visitors to Aranmula can watch artisans at small workshops, buy certified mirrors and hear the village stories that connect the craft to temple ritual and the famous Vallamkali (boat‑race) tradition.

Because production is small and specialised, authenticity matters: genuine mirrors carry community certification markings introduced in recent years; confirm provenance and buy from recognised artisans or authorised outlets.

Background

Aranmula’s metal mirror tradition is centuries old and passed down inside specialist Vishwakarma/Viswabrahmana families. The mirrors are produced from a secret alloy and a labour‑intensive polishing process that produces a front‑surface reflection, unlike ordinary glass mirrors.

The Aranmula Kannadi holds ritual and cultural significance locally — historically used for deity viewing and considered auspicious in households. The craft received Geographical Indication protection in 2004–05 to safeguard its identity and local makers.

What to see and do

Visit small family workshops and authorised handicraft outlets in Aranmula and nearby Malakkara or Edayaranmula to watch casting, finishing and the final hand‑polishing stages. Artisans often explain motifs and frame designs; workshops are small and visits are best during daytime.

Combine a mirror visit with the Parthasarathy Temple precinct and, if your travel dates match, the Aranmula Vallamkali (boat race) and temple festivals. Buy mirrors directly from makers or DTPC/authorised outlets to ensure authenticity and ask for provenance details.

Plan your visit

Getting there: Aranmula is a temple village in Pathanamthitta district. The nearest railway station is Chengannur (about 9–11 km); the nearest major airport commonly used is Trivandrum International Airport (about 117 km by road). Reach Aranmula by road from Pathanamthitta and Chengannur; local buses and taxis serve the village.

Best time and access: The dry, comfortable season is October–March; the Onam season (August–September) is important for local river pageantry (Uthrattathi Vallamkali) but dates move with the Malayalam calendar — check current festival dates before you go. Workshops may be affected by local festival rituals or temple schedules, so confirm opening times before travel.

Etiquette & good to know

  • Buy from recognised artisan workshops or authorised outlets — ask for provenance; many genuine mirrors carry community authentication markings introduced in recent years (check for the maker’s mark/hologram and certificate).
  • Mirrors are handcrafted and made to order; expect lead times and handle pieces carefully — confirm shipping/packaging before purchase.
  • Respect temple etiquette at Aranmula Parthasarathy Temple (dress and photography rules) when combining a craft visit with temple sightseeing.
  • Ask artisans about their process and history — direct support (buying or commissioning work) helps sustain the community; confirm prices and delivery details before payment.
