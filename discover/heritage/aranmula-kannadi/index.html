Aranmula Kannadi — the Metal Mirror

ആറന്മുള കണ്ണാടി

A hand-cast metal-alloy mirror made only in Aranmula

The Aranmula kannadi is a polished metal-alloy mirror made by a small number of families in Aranmula, a temple town on the Pamba river. Unlike glass mirrors it reflects from the front surface, so there is no ghost image.

The exact alloy and polishing method are traditionally guarded as a family craft. Aranmula kannadi holds a Geographical Indication tag and is used as a lucky object in ceremonies.

Highlights

  • Front-surface metal mirror with no ghost image
  • Aranmula Parthasarathy Temple
  • Pamba river and the Uthrattathi boat race
  • Vallasadya feast

History & significance

Why Aranmula Kannadi — the Metal Mirror matters

One of Kerala's most distinctive crafts, tied to the Aranmula Parthasarathy Temple and the ritual life of the town.

  1. Traditional origin

    Local legend places the craft among artisans brought to serve the Aranmula temple.

  2. 20th century

    The craft survives through a few families and cooperative bodies.

  3. 2000s

    Aranmula Kannadi receives Geographical Indication protection, helping buyers identify authentic pieces.

Good to know

  • The mirror is front-surfaced, which gives a sharp, undistorted reflection.
  • It is made by a small number of artisan families; production is slow.
  • One of the items placed in the ashtamangalyam set, auspicious articles used in ceremonies.
  • Real pieces are sold with tags or certificates; check the seller's credentials.

Legend & folklore

The Mirror of the Temple

Tradition says the first kannadi was made by a group of skilled metalworkers who came to Aranmula to serve the Parthasarathy Temple, and that the secret alloy was entrusted to their families.

Another story says the king of Travancore valued the mirror so highly that he placed it among the auspicious items offered at royal ceremonies.

Oral accounts differ; origin is traditional, not documented history.

Cultural & spiritual meaning

What it means

The mirror is treated as an auspicious object that brings clarity and good fortune into a home.

Visit a workshop

At a family workshop you can see polishing, which takes patient, repeated work.

Attend the Aranmula temple festival season

The town comes alive during the Uthrattathi boat race and the Vallasadya feast; check dates.

Best for: Craft-lovers and reflective travellers.

  • Handle the mirror gently; scratches are hard to repair.
  • Respect the workshop's request about photographing the process.

Shop & buy

Where to buy traditional pieces

Prefer co-operatives, government emporiums and workshops you can visit. Always confirm the shop is open and current before travelling.

  • Aranmula Vishwabrahma Handicrafts Co-operative / Aranmula Kannadi workshops

    Co-operative

    Pathanamthitta

    Buy from certified outlets with a Geographical Indication tag or certificate (verify the current society and shops).

  • Kairali — Kerala State Handicrafts Apex Co-operative Society emporiums

    Co-operative

    Statewide

    Government-linked craft emporiums in major towns; fixed prices and craft-certified items.

Buying tips

  • Insist on a tag or certificate and a written guarantee.
  • Prices are high; very cheap 'Aranmula mirrors' are imitations.
  • Take the piece in protective packing for travel.

Plan your visit

Best time
October to March; boat race and temple festivals fall in August–September.
How to reach
Aranmula is on the Pamba river near Chengannur; the nearest railhead is Chengannur (CNGR), about 10 km away.
Suggested time
Half a day

Etiquette & good to know

  • Buy only from certified workshops or cooperatives.
  • Do not bargain in temple precincts.
